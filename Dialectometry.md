## 🌍 What I Mean by Dialectometry  
Dialectometry examines **how far** linguistic varieties diverge.  
It goes beyond descriptive dialectology and uses:

- **Distance matrices (Levenshtein, ALINE, feature-based)**
- **Cluster analysis**
- **Multidimensional scaling (NMDS)**
- **Geographical mapping**
- **Ecological correlations (altitude, climate, landuse)**

This page summarizes the tools, methods, and projects I work on.

---

## 🌱 Eco-Dialectometry *(a term I introduce)*  
I coined **Eco-Dialectometry** to describe an approach that integrates:

- ecological variables  
- cultural landscapes  
- environmental constraints  

into dialect variation studies.

Examples of ecological factors I use:

- altitude  
- distance to rivers  
- vegetation & landcover  
- climate  
- rural vs. urban settlement patterns  

This approach helps explain **why** dialects diverge the way they do — not just *how much* they differ.

## 📈 Methods I Regularly Use

### • **Levenshtein Distance**
To measure wordlist similarities across villages/locations.  
Often my first step in analyzing dialect variation.

### • **NMDS (Non-metric Multidimensional Scaling)**
Reveals hidden structures and clusters.  
Especially powerful with 20+ locations.

### • **Cluster Analysis**
Ward / UPGMA for:
- hierarchical trees  
- grouping dialects  
- visualizing ecological regions  

### • **Mantel Test**
To test:
- linguistic distance × geographic distance  
- linguistic distance × ecological distance  

### • **Heatmaps & matrices**
For intuitive visualizations of:
- lexical distances  
- segment correspondences  
- frequency patterns
